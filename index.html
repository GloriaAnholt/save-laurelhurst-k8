Portland Public Schools’ Rightsizing plans would turn seven K-8 schools into K-5s and send their 6th through 8th graders to middle schools. PPS says stand-alone middle schools are more cost-effective and more equitable, but the numbers say otherwise.

I’m a parent of a Laurelhurst student, and this website is my1 attempt to make sense of the data. I can’t speak for other schools’ families, so I use my kid’s school as the worked example. I went through PPS’s own budgets and reports, state test data, census data, newspaper archives, and more to check the case for change.

The answer is clear: Tell the PPS Board to vote NO on Rightsizing. Demand a better plan with clear implementation details.

  • Would the new K-5s be sustainable?

    6 of 7

    K-8s PPS wants to convert would start out below its own 360-student sustainability threshold as K-5s. The four K-8s it keeps are already below their threshold, too.

    See the plan →

  • Do K-8 middle grades really cost more?

    $7,226

    per middle schooler in teacher cost at Sunnyside, by PPS’s own math. That’s below PPS’s $8,750 K-8 average. The extra cost sits in a few small programs.

    See the math →

  • Does converting a K-8 add new costs?

    $38K–$77K

    a year in new bus routes to take Laurelhurst’s middle schoolers to Mt Tabor, after Oregon pays its share. PPS has no general bus routes to Laurelhurst today.

    See the math →

  • How do K-8 middle graders do on state tests?

    78%

    of Laurelhurst and Beverly Cleary 6th–8th graders met the state reading standard in 2024–25, tied for the highest of any PPS neighborhood school. All of PPS: 56%.

  • What does research say about K-8 schools?

    127% larger

    math losses for students who were already behind. In a peer-reviewed Harvard study, moving to middle school in 6th grade cost them more than twice as much as their classmates.

    See the research →

  • What happened to the K-8s PPS already converted?

    5 of 14

    K-8s that PPS has converted to K-5s since 2016 are on its closure list in both plans.

  • Can Laurelhurst fill its middle grades?

    16%

    more school-age kids lived in Laurelhurst in 2020 than in 2000, rising from 795 to 922, even as the neighborhood’s older population grew.

    See the numbers →

  • Do neighborhood families choose Laurelhurst?

    85%

    of PPS students living in Laurelhurst’s area attend Laurelhurst, 14th of 74 PPS schools. The district average is 70%.

    ~350

    students would be left at Laurelhurst as a K-5, below PPS’s own 360-student threshold. The plan moves a shortfall instead of fixing it.
