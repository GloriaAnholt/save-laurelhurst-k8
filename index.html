Portland Public Schools’ Rightsizing plans would turn seven K-8 schools into K-5s and send their 6th through 8th graders to middle schools. PPS says stand-alone middle schools are more cost-effective and more equitable, but the numbers say otherwise.

I’m a parent of a Laurelhurst student, and this website is my1 attempt to make sense of the data. I can’t speak for other schools’ families, so I use my own school as the worked example: I went through PPS’s own budgets, state test data, and bus records to check the case for the change. Every number below links to its source.

Which K-8s does PPS keep?

PPS says a K-8 needs at least 500 students to be sustainable. Only three K-8s are forecast to reach that in 2026-27, and both plans convert all three. Every K-8 that PPS keeps is forecast below PPS’s own minimum of 500. And once their middle graders leave, six of the seven converted K-8s would start out below PPS’s 360-student minimum for a K-5, given their current boundaries.2
