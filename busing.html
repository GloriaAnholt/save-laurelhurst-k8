How we did the math

The bus budget

PPS budgets $28.8 million in 2026-27 for contracted yellow-bus service (budget line 533110). That excludes taxis ($2.9M), TriMet passes ($2.1M) and PPS’s own transportation staff. All student transportation together is $48.9 million in 2026-27, up from $44.1 million the year before, so busing was not cut in this budget.

Counting routes

PPS posts bus schedules school by school. Parent volunteers counted the route files for all 65 schools listed: 223 morning routes and 226 afternoon routes. Laurelhurst has none. Mt Tabor has five.

This counts route listings, not buses. One bus often serves several schools and shows up in each school’s list, and a 2020 PPS staff report counted 131 big-bus routes. If there are fewer buses than listings, each one costs more than $128,000, and our estimate is low.

What PPS pays after the state

Oregon reimburses districts 70%, 80% or 90% of approved bus costs, depending on how a district’s cost per student ranks statewide (ORS 327.013). PPS ranks 59th of 100, below the 80% cutoff at rank 80, so it gets 70% back and pays 30% itself.

Who would ride

Oregon’s rule and PPS’s guidelines call for busing middle schoolers who live 1.5 miles or more from school, measured along streets and paths. Measured that way, about 29% of Laurelhurst’s attendance area is more than 1.5 miles from Mt Tabor. In a straight line, only about 2% is. PPS hasn’t published a transportation plan for this change, so we assumed one or two routes.

What this leaves out

  • Buses for students who qualify under PPS’s hazard rules (busy streets without shoulders or sidewalks), which can add riders who live closer than 1.5 miles.
  • Families who start driving, and the traffic at Mt Tabor at drop-off.
  • Ride time. A route that picks up across a large area is longer than the straight-line distance suggests.
