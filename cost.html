What does a K-8 student really cost?

PPS says K-8 middle grades cost about $1,750 more per student than regular middle schools (~$8,750 vs. ~$7,000), but that figure is an average that hides a wide range. Using PPS’s own staffing numbers, this extra cost is concentrated in a few small programs, yet PPS’s plans convert the lowest-cost K-8s while keeping or expanding three of the five most expensive.

PPS’s numbers count only teachers,1 which makes a K-8’s middle grades look far more expensive than they are. Count the whole school and the K-8 premium mostly disappears.

Fact check

Affluent neighborhoods tend to send 22% of their kids to private schools. At Laurelhurst, it’s only 18%.

If Laurelhurst’s neighborhood sent their kids to private schools at the average for its income, 11 to 28 fewer students would be in PPS. That’s $122,000–$311,000 a year in state funding lost.4

At the low end, PPS keeps a little over half of what it expects to save. At the high end, converting Laurelhurst costs PPS money. And because Oregon funds districts on the higher of this or last year’s enrollment numbers, a loss wouldn’t show up in PPS’s budget until a year after families leave.

K-5 conversions don’t save on overhead

Teachers’ salaries aren’t a school’s only staffing cost. PPS also pays for principals, office staff, counselors, and librarians. Converting a K-8 to a K-5 moves students between two campuses that both stay open, so PPS still pays to run both.

→
After conversion, my estimate ~15.3 About the same, or a little more: Laurelhurst as a K-5 (6.5) and a larger Mt Tabor (8.8)5
  • Laurelhurst keeps its staffing overhead. As a K-5 it still needs a principal, an office, and the rest of a school’s staff.
  • Mt Tabor adds to its own teaching costs. It would grow from about 415 students to about 641, and its staff grows with enrollment.
  • Overhead follows the number of students, not the number of grades. In PPS’s budget, K-5s, K-8s, and middle schools of similar size require about the same amount of non-teaching staff. Moving the students between the two campuses doesn’t shrink this overhead.
  • Laurelhurst would get less efficient. Today it has the leanest non-teaching staffing of any PPS K-8, at about $2,100 per student. As a K-5 of 348 students, I estimate it’s closer to $3,000 per student.

Regardless of how you count the costs, PPS’s projected savings are too small and too uncertain to justify converting strong neighborhood K-8s.

  • PPS, 2026–27 Proposed Budget, Volume 2 (updated May 6, 2026). School summary table, p. 5 (dollars per student and students per FTE by school), and individual school pages (staffing by position type, salaries, and payroll costs).
  • PPS, “Rightsizing: Comparing three scenarios,” updated October 4, 2026. List of grade-level changes, identical in Scenarios A and B: Beverly Cleary, Laurelhurst, Bridger, Sunnyside, Faubion, Vernon, and César Chávez grades 6–8 move to middle schools; MLC becomes a K-8.

How the numbers were calculated

  • Cost per student = grades 6–8 teacher FTE × $160,000 ÷ grades 6–8 enrollment, using PPS’s figures. For the 15 middle schools this reproduces PPS’s published numbers exactly (for example, Mt Tabor: 17.6 × $160,000 ÷ 415 = $6,785.54). For K-8s I applied the same formula to PPS’s “Actual 6–8 Teacher FTE in a K-8” column.
  • Where $160,000 comes from: PPS does not say. It is the cost per FTE implied by PPS’s own subsidy table (for example, ACCESS: 1.9 FTE = $304,000). PPS notes the comparison covers “General 6–12 Instructor FTE, not all funds nor positions.”
  • Whole-school cost per student is PPS’s own “2026–27 $ per student” from the budget’s school summary table (Vol. 2, p. 5): each school’s General Fund, Title I, and grant dollars divided by its enrollment, excluding special education, ESL, and nutrition. Averages are weighted by 2026–27 enrollment from each school’s budget page. The 12 K-8s exclude MLC, a K-12 alternative program. Lane Middle School in the budget appears as Brentwood in PPS’s October presentation. Another Laurelhurst parent first ran this enrollment-weighted comparison; I re-ran it from the budget book and got the same results.
  • Full middle-grades cost (my estimate) starts from each K-8’s whole-school budget. Grades 6–8 get their own teachers (PPS’s grades 6–8 teacher count × the school’s own average cost per position) plus a share of everything else (administration, counselors, office, library, other staff, and supplies) in proportion to enrollment. The result is divided by grades 6–8 enrollment. It assumes teachers and other staff cost the school’s average. For Laurelhurst the estimate is about $11,004; at a flat $160,000 per teacher it is about $10,600, and splitting overhead by teacher count instead of enrollment gives about $11,300. A middle school’s whole-school cost is already its middle-grades cost.
  • Extra cost (“subsidy”) = (actual FTE − formula FTE) × $160,000, as published by PPS; total 21.1 FTE = $3,376,000.
  • Tiers are the natural breaks in cost per student: no K-8 falls between $7,779 and $8,209, or between $9,020 and $9,205. Tier averages are weighted by enrollment (tier teacher FTE × $160,000 ÷ tier enrollment). PPS’s $8,750 K-8 average = 110.3 FTE × $160,000 ÷ 2,017 students.
  • “The plan” compares each tier’s programs against the scenario grade-level changes. Dollar amounts “untouched” are the PPS subsidy figures for programs that remain K-8 (Astor $320K, Skyline $192K, MLC $416K).

Limitations and context

  • One average cost for every teacher. Actual costs vary with experience and education. In the 2026–27 budget, the average cost (salaries + payroll costs) per school-funded position ranges from about $145,000 (Faubion) to $173,000 (Kellogg). Using each school’s own average instead of $160,000: Laurelhurst $8,803, Mt Tabor $7,113. PPS doesn’t publish position-level costs.
  • Teachers only. PPS’s comparison leaves out principals, counselors, office, and library staff. Standalone middle schools fund these themselves; K-8s share them across nine grades. Counting the whole school, the 2026–27 budget shows Laurelhurst at $10,022 per student (the lowest of any K-8) vs. a middle school average of $10,776, and Laurelhurst has the most students per staff member of any K-8 or middle school (13.7).
  • Different missions. ACCESS is a talented-and-gifted program, Odyssey and Winterhaven admit by lottery, MLC is an alternative school, and Bridger and Sunnyside are focus-option schools. César Chávez and Astor serve higher-poverty communities. Higher cost may be justified for some programs; the point is that PPS has not shown why it converts lower-cost K-8s while keeping higher-cost ones.
  • Cost is not outcomes. This page does not measure results. For reference, 2024–25 state test results (Oregon Department of Education) show Laurelhurst grades 6–8 at 78.2% proficient in English and 65.6% in math, vs. 56.4% and 46.9% for all PPS students in those grades. See test scores →
  • Enrollment and FTE figures are as published by PPS in October 2026; small differences from other PPS reports reflect different snapshot dates.
